\textbf{\Large\begin{center}
    {Learning By Doing: Externalities}
\end{center}}
\vspace*{2cm}
Consider an economy described as follows:
\begin{align*}
    u(c_t) &=\dfrac{c_t^{1-1/\theta}}{1-1/\theta} \\
    Y_t &= K_t^\alpha (h_t L_t)^{1-\alpha} \\
    k_{t+1} &= (1 - \delta)k_t + i_t \\
    h_t &= b k_t , b > 0, L_t = L = \text{constant}, \forall t \\
\end{align*}
Assume discount factor, \(\beta\), is in (0, 1).

\problem{1.}Describe how human capital, $h_t$, accumulates in this economy. Do agents invest in improving $h_t$? Do
you believe centeralized, and decenteralized problems, will have the same outcome in this economy?\\\\\\
Agents do not invest in improving human capital in this model.
The human capital in this model is increased as physical capital per worker increases $(h_t=b k_t)$.
Meaning that workers' human capital is increased when more physical capital is allocated to them.\\\\
The social planner's does not come to the same result as decenteralized problem,
increase in human capital is an externality in this model.
Thus, we expect that the investment in physical capital in the equilibrium would be less than the social optimum.

\problem{2.}Write and solve the social planner's problem. What is the growth rate of consumption in this economy?
What is the optimal saving rate (assume a linear savings function)?\\\\\\
The resource constraint is as follows:
\begin{align*}
    k_{t+1} -  k_t = k_t^{\alpha} h_t^{1-\alpha} -\delta k_t - c_t
\end{align*}\\
Plugging the problems assumption $(h_t=bk_t)$ in it, we will reach: 
\begin{align*}
    k_{t+1} -  k_t & = k_t^{\alpha}\;(bk_t)^{1-\alpha} -\delta k_t - c_t\\
    & = k_t\;b^{1-\alpha} -\delta k_t - c_t\\
\end{align*}
The social planner wants to maximize the discounted utility subject to the resource constraint, by choosing the consumption of current and capital of the next period!
So, its problem would be:
\begin{align*}
    \max_{c_t, k_{t+1}} \; \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \beta^{t} u(c_t) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad
    k_{t+1} -  k_t = k_t\;b^{1-\alpha} -\delta k_t - c_t
\end{align*}\\
Writing the Lagrangian, we have:
\begin{align*}
    \mathcal{L} = \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \beta^{t} u(c_t) + \sum_{t=0}^{\infty}
    \mu_t\biggl[(1 + b^{1-\alpha} - \delta) k_t - c_t - k_{t+1}\biggr]
\end{align*}\\
Taking the FOC, we get:
\begin{align*}
    \rond{\mathcal{L}}{c_t} &= \beta^{t} u^\prime(c_t) - \mu_t = 0 \tag{\ones}\\
    \rond{\mathcal{L}}{k_{t+1}} &= -\mu_t + \mu_{t+1} (1 + b^{1-\alpha}-\delta) = 0 \tag{\twos}
\end{align*}\\
Plugging $\mu_{t} \And \mu_{t+1}$ from \ones\; into \twos, we will reach the Euler equation:
\begin{align*}
    -\mu_t + \mu_{t+1} (1 + b^{1-\alpha}-\delta) &= 0 \\
    -\beta^{t} u^\prime(c_t) + \beta^{t+1} u^\prime(c_{t+1}) (1 + b^{1-\alpha}-\delta) &= 0\\
    \beta^{t+1} u^\prime(c_{t+1}) (1 + b^{1-\alpha}-\delta) &= \beta^{t} u^\prime(c_t)\\
    \implies\dfrac{u^\prime(c_{t+1}) }{u^\prime(c_t)} &= \dfrac{1}{\beta (1 + b^{1-\alpha}-\delta)}\; \checkmark\\
\end{align*}
Previously we had:
$$u(c_t) = \dfrac{c_t^{1-1/\theta}}{1-1/\theta} \implies u'(c_t)=c_t^{-1/\theta}$$\\
Plugging into the Euler equation, we get:
\begin{align*}
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} &= \bigl[\beta(1 + b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)\bigr]^{\theta} \tag{\threes}
\end{align*}\\
Assuming a linear saving function with rate $s$, we have:
\begin{align*}
    c_t &= (1-s)(1 + b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)k_t \tag{$1$}\\
    c_{t+1} &= (1-s)(1 + b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)k_{t+1} \tag{$2$}
\end{align*}\\
Also we have:
\begin{align*}
    k_{t+1}=s(k_tb^{1-\alpha}-\delta k_t)=s(b^{1-\alpha}-\delta)k_t \tag{$3$}
\end{align*}
Plugging $(1)$ and $(2)$ into the \threes, we get:
\begin{align*}
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} &= \bigl[\beta(1 + b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)\bigr]^{\theta} \\
    \frac{\cancel{(1-s)(1 + b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)}\;k_{t+1}}{\cancel{(1-s)(1 + b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)}\;k_t} &= \bigl[\beta(1 + b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)\bigr]^{\theta} \\
\end{align*}\\
Plugging $(3)$ into the last equation and solving for $s$, we finally reach the optimal saving rate, which is:
$$s = \beta^{\theta} (1 + b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)^{\theta-1}$$

\problem{3.}Show that the household budget constraint can be written as:
$$c_t + k_{t+1} + b_{t+1} = w_t h_t + (1 + r_t - \delta) k_t + (1 + \underbrace{R_t}_{\text{ or } R_{t-1}}  ) b_t$$\\
where $b+t$ is a risk-free bond with return $R_t$ (determined endogenously).
What is the arbitrage condition between risk-free bonds and capital?
Use the FOCs of the household to find Euler equation.
Using the firm’s profit maximizing behavior, what is the equilibrium interest rate for physical capital and the wage rate?
What is the growth rate of consumption?\\\\\\
The income sources for households are effective wages $w_th_t$, return on capital $r_tk_t$ and return on bonds $R_tb_t$. In each period, the household can either consume the income $c_t$,
or invest in the physical capital $i_t^k$,
or buy the government bonds $i_t^b$.
So we can write the household budget constraint as:
\begin{align*}
    c_t + i_t^k + i_t^b &= w_t h_t + r_t k_t + R_t b_t \\
    c_t + (k_{t+1}-k_t+\delta k_t) + i_t^b &= w_t h_t + r_t k_t + R_t b_t \tag{Using $i_t^k=k_{t+1}-k_t+\delta k_t$}\\
    c_t + (k_{t+1}-k_t+\delta k_t) + (b_{t+1}-b_t) &= w_t h_t + r_t k_t + R_t b_t \tag{Using $i_t^b=b_{t+1}-b_t$}\\
    c_t + k_{t+1} + b_{t+1} &= w_t h_t + (1 + r_t - \delta) k_t + (1 + R_t) b_t\; \checkmark \tag{\ones}\\
\end{align*}\\
In order to not having arbitrage, the following equation of return of capital and bond must hold.
Otherwise the agents will prefer one type of investment to the other.
\begin{align*}
    R_t = r_t - \delta
\end{align*}
Plugging into (\ones), we get:
\begin{align*}
    c_t + k_{t+1} + b_{t+1} &= w_t h_t + (1 + R_t) (k_t+b_t)=w_t h_t + \bigl(1 + (r_t-\delta)\bigr) (k_t+b_t)\\
\end{align*}
The no-arbitrage condition implies that there is no difference in investing in capital or bonds,
So basically we can define assest in period $t$ as: $a_t=k_t+b_t$. Plugging into the previous equation, we get:
\begin{align*}
    c_t + k_{t+1} + b_{t+1} = w_t h_t + (1 + R_t) (a_t) = w_t h_t + \bigl(1 + (r_t-\delta)\bigr) (a_t)\\
\end{align*}
The household is choosing its consumption in the current period and its asset in the next period to maximize its aggregate utility subject to the budget constraint.
So its problem would be:
\begin{align*}
    \max_{c_t, a_{t+1}} \; \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \beta^{t} u(c_t) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad
    c_t + k_{t+1} + b_{t+1} = w_t h_t + (1 + R_t) (a_t)
\end{align*}\\
So the lagrangian for this problem is:
\begin{align*}
    \mathcal{L} = \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \beta^{t} u(c_t)
    + \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \mu_t \biggl[w_th_t + (1 + R_t) a_t - c_t - a_{t+1}\biggr]
\end{align*}
Taking FOC, we get:
\begin{align*}
    \rond{\mathcal{L}}{c_t} &= \beta^t u^\prime(c_t) - \mu_t = 0 \tag{1}\\
    \rond{\mathcal{L}}{a_{t+1}} &= -\mu_t + (1+R_t)\mu_{t+1} = 0 \tag{2}\\
\end{align*}
Plugging $\mu_{t} \And \mu_{t+1}$ from $(1)$ into $(2)$,
we will reach the Euler equation:
\begin{align*}
   -\mu_t + (1+R_t)\mu_{t+1} & = 0 \\
   -\beta^{t} u^\prime(c_{t}) + (1+R_{t})\beta^{t+1} u^\prime(c_{t+1}) & = 0 \\
    (1+R_{t+1})\beta^{t+1} u^\prime(c_{t}) & = \beta^{t} u^\prime(c_{t}) \\
    \implies\dfrac{u^\prime(c_{t+1}) }{u^\prime(c_t)} &= \dfrac{1}{\beta (1 + R_{t})}\; \checkmark
\end{align*}\\
Plugging  $u'(c) = c^{-1/\theta}$ into the Euler equation:
\begin{align*}
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} = (\beta (R_t+1))^{\theta}
\end{align*}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
The firm problem is to maximize the profit, choosing capital and labor:
\begin{align*}
    \max_{K, L} \; \Pi = K_t^{\alpha} (h_t L_t)^{1-\alpha} - w_th_tL_t - r_tK_t
\end{align*}\\
Taking the first order condition we have:
\begin{align*}
    \rond{\Pi}{K}&= \alpha K_t^{\alpha-1} (h_tL_t)^{1-\alpha} - r_t = 0\\
    \implies r_t &= \alpha K_t^{\alpha-1} (h_tL_t)^{1-\alpha}\\
    &= \alpha K_t^{\alpha-1} L_t^{-(\alpha-1)} h_t^{1-\alpha}\\
    &= \alpha k_t^{\alpha-1} h_t^{1-\alpha}
\end{align*}
\begin{align*}
    \rond{\Pi}{L} &= (1-\alpha) K_t^{\alpha}h_t^{1-\alpha}L_t^{-\alpha} - w_t h_t = 0\\
    \implies  w_t &= (1-\alpha)K_t^{\alpha}h_t^{-\alpha}L_t^{-\alpha}\\
    &= (1-\alpha)k_t^{\alpha}h_t^{-\alpha}\\
\end{align*}\\
From the previous part, we have the Euler equation, substituting $R_t,r_t$ into it, we get the consumption growth:\\
\begin{align*}
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} &= \bigl[\beta (1 + R_t)\bigr]^{\theta} \tag{Using $R_t = r_t - \delta$}\\
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} &= \bigl[\beta (1 + (r_t - \delta))\bigr]^{\theta} \tag{Using $r_t = \alpha k_t^{\alpha-1} h_t^{1-\alpha}$}\\
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} &= \bigl[\beta (1 + (\alpha k_t^{\alpha-1} h_t^{1-\alpha} - \delta))\bigr]^{\theta} \tag{Using $h_t = b k_t$}\\
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} &= \bigl[\beta (1 + (\alpha b_t - \delta))\bigr]^{\theta}\\
\end{align*}

\problem{4.}Compare the growth rate in centeralized and decenteralized problems. How are they different, and why?\\\\\\
The growth rate of consumption in the centeral planner problem is as follows:
\begin{align*}
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} &= \bigl[\beta(1 + b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)\bigr]^{\theta} 
\end{align*}\\
The growth rate of consumption in the decenteralized problem is as follows:
\begin{align*}
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} = \bigl[\beta (1 + \alpha b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)\bigr]^{\theta} 
\end{align*}\\

We know that $\alpha < 1$, implying that the growth rate in the centeral planner problem is higher than the decenteralized problem.


It is due to positive externality of learning by doing, which is not considered in the decenteralized problem but is consiedered in the centeral planner's problem.

\problem{5.}Suppose the government subsidizes the private cost of capital for firms.
In other words, firms must pay $(1 - \tau)r_t$ instead of $r_t$ for each unit of capital employed.
The government pays for this subsidy via lump sum taxes, $T$, on individuals.
Solve the decenteralized problem in this economy.\\\\\\
Rerwiting the budget constraint and implementing the tax, we have:
\begin{align*}
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} = c_t + k_{t+1} + b_{t+1} = w_th_t + (1 + r_t - \delta) k_t + (1+R_t) b_t - T_t
\end{align*}
The FOCs of the centeralized problem and no-arbitrage will not change due to the lump sum tax.\\
So the growth rate of consumption will still be the same as the last part:
\begin{align}
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} &= \bigl[\beta(1 + b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)\bigr]^{\theta} 
\end{align}\\
On the other hand, firm's problem has changed due to the subsidy on the private cost of capital.\\
Rewriting the firm's problem:
\begin{align*}
    \max_{K, L} \; \Pi = K_t^{\alpha} (h_t L_t)^{1-\alpha} - w_th_tL_t - (1-\tau)r_tK_t
\end{align*}\\
Taking the first order condition we have:
\begin{align*}
    \rond{\Pi}{K}&= \alpha K_t^{\alpha-1} (h_tL_t)^{1-\alpha} - (1-\tau)r_t = 0\\
    \implies r_t &= \dfrac{\alpha}{(1-\tau)} K_t^{\alpha-1} (h_tL_t)^{1-\alpha}\\
    &= \dfrac{\alpha}{(1-\tau)} K_t^{\alpha-1} L_t^{-(\alpha-1)} h_t^{1-\alpha}\\
    &= \dfrac{\alpha}{1-\tau} k_t^{\alpha-1} h_t^{1-\alpha}
\end{align*}
\begin{align*}
    \rond{\Pi}{L} &= (1-\alpha) K_t^{\alpha}h_t^{1-\alpha}L_t^{-\alpha} - w_t h_t = 0\\
    \implies  w_t &= (1-\alpha)K_t^{\alpha}h_t^{-\alpha}L_t^{-\alpha}\\
    &= (1-\alpha)k_t^{\alpha}h_t^{-\alpha}\\
\end{align*}\\\\
From the previous part, we have the Euler equation, substituting $R_t,r_t,b$ into it; we get the consumption growth:\\
\begin{align*}
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} &= \bigl[\beta (1 + R_t)\bigr]^{\theta} \tag{Using $R_t = r_t - \delta$}\\
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} &= \bigl[\beta (1 + (r_t - \delta))\bigr]^{\theta} \tag{Using $r_t = \dfrac{\alpha}{1-\tau} k_t^{\alpha-1} h_t^{1-\alpha}$}\\
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} &= \bigl[\beta (1 + \dfrac{\alpha}{1-\tau}\;k_t^{\alpha-1} h_t^{1-\alpha} - \delta)\bigr]^{\theta} \tag{Using $h_t = b k_t$}\\
    \frac{c_{t+1}}{c_t} &= \bigl[\beta (1 + \dfrac{\alpha}{1-\tau} b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)\bigr]^{\theta}\\
\end{align*}

\problem{6.}What is the optimal level of $\tau$ (for which the centeralized and decenteralized problems coincide)? Why does
the government want to subsidize investment in this model?\\\\\\
For centeral planner's problem growth rate with subsidy to coincide with the decenteralized problem, we should have:
\begin{align*}
    \bigl[\beta(1 + b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)\bigr]^{\theta}  &=  \bigl[\beta (1 + \dfrac{\alpha}{1-\tau} b^{1-\alpha} - \delta)\bigr]^{\theta}\\
    \implies \qquad 1  &=  \dfrac{\alpha}{1-\tau}\\ 
    \implies  \;1-\tau  &=  \alpha\\
    \implies  \qquad \tau  &=  \alpha +1 
\end{align*}\\

The government is willing to subsidize investment because firms do not invest in physical capital at the social optimal level.
The social optimal level in this model is higher than the firm's optimal level because of the learning by doing positive externality.
So the government is willing to increase the investment level to the social optimal level by giving subsidy on the investment.
